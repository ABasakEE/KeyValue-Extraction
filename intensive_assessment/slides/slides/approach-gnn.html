Approach 3: Delaunay word graph + Edge-GAT

Our hypothesis was that how a word sits relative to its neighbours would carry over to new templates better than its absolute position on the page.

Graph

Nodes = words, features = 768-D LiLT states (cached). Edges = Delaunay triangulation of word centres; optional pruning of edges that cross ruled lines (OpenCV).

RSA-GNN tagger

768 → 256, 1–3 Edge-GAT layers, 4 heads × 64, dropout 0.1, MLP to 13 BIO tags. Cross-entropy loss.

NER-fused joint GNN

Adds an 8-D regex type one-hot (date, money, ID, name…). Node head: key / value / header / other. Edge head: MLP on [hᵢ ‖ hⱼ ‖ eᵢⱼ]. Loss CE + 1.5 × focal.

Here each word connects to its Delaunay neighbours, and every edge carries the same 16 spatial features as the linking head. Because the attention score uses the edge features, the model can learn, for example, that a word just to the right of a key matters more than one far below it. The fused version adds a cheap entity-type hint from regular expressions and predicts links directly on the graph edges.
